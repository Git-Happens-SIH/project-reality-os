The gap

Planning and execution still live in different worlds

Site engineers write reports in the field. Planners update schedules days later. By the time a delay is visible on the Gantt, cost has already compounded.

Field

Notes trapped on phones

Daily reports sit in chat threads, PDFs, or paper pads. Photos lack task context. When connectivity fails, yesterday’s progress never leaves the site cabin.

Planning

Manual matching, stale truth

Someone retypes field notes into Primavera, MS Project, or Excel. Matching unstructured text to WBS codes is slow, error-prone, and always behind the site.

Leadership

Decisions on last week’s data

Delay alerts arrive after the window to recover. Stakeholders argue about which spreadsheet is current. The programme looks fine until it suddenly is not.

Offline-first by design

Remote corridors, tunnels, and low-signal zones are normal for infrastructure work. Reporting should not wait for a bar of reception.

Write reports with zero connectivity. They save locally and sync the moment the device finds a signal. No data loss. No “try again later.” Drafts stay on the device until the upload confirms.

  • Local-first storage with conflict-safe sync when back online
  • Photo attachments queued with the same report payload
  • Clear sync status so engineers know what is still pending

Works in remote sites, tunnels, and low-signal zones.

One source of truth

Every approved report updates the live schedule. Delay alerts surface automatically. Confidence tiers keep uncertain matches out of the “confirmed” column.

Live project dashboard — Confirmed / Review Needed / Possible Risk tiers

No more spreadsheet archaeology. Planners and leads share the same confirmed progress view, with provenance attached to each task.

Confirmed High-confidence matches approved by a planner — safe to treat as programme truth.
Review needed Ambiguous links or incomplete notes. Held until a human decides.
Possible risk Conflicts, delays, or low confidence. Surfaced early — never silently written.

Human authority stays on top of every automated suggestion

Four controls that keep AI helpful without handing it the pen. Scroll — each layer stacks over the last.

01 Approval

Planner in the loop

AI suggests matches and flags risks. A responsible human always approves or rejects. Nothing is auto-written into the schedule — the planner keeps final authority on every change that hits the live plan.

Human sign-off required 1 / 4
02 Scoring

Confidence tiers

Every match carries a clear score: Confirmed, Review Needed, or Possible Risk. Low-confidence items never slip through quietly — they surface for review before they can distort progress reporting.

No silent low-confidence writes 2 / 4
03 History

Audit trail

Who reported, who approved, when it synced. Full history stays attached to the task — so when questions come up weeks later, context is still sitting on the record, not lost in chat threads.

Full provenance per task 3 / 4
04 Ownership

Data stays yours

Project data is isolated by organisation and project code. Site notes are never used to train models without explicit consent — your programme remains your programme.
